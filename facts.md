## Signs of stress

Sources: [WHO: stress](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/stress) and
[NHS Every Mind Matters: stress](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-health-issues/stress/),
checked 2026-11-11.

- WHO: stress is a state of worry or mental tension caused by a difficult situation, and a natural
  response that prompts us to deal with challenges. A little helps; too much can harm physical
  and mental health.
- NHS: under pressure the body releases adrenaline, the "fight or flight" hormone, which gives a
  short boost to act quickly.
- Common signs (WHO, NHS): trouble relaxing, irritability, worry, trouble concentrating,
  headaches or body pain, upset stomach, trouble sleeping, eating more or less than usual,
  drinking or smoking more, avoiding people.
- WHO: long-lasting stress can worsen existing health problems, increase use of alcohol, tobacco
  and other substances, and cause or worsen anxiety and depression.

## When it may be more than stress

Checked 2026-11-11.

| Condition | What the official source says |
| --- | --- |
| Burn-out | An "occupational phenomenon", not a medical condition: chronic workplace stress that hasn't been managed, with exhaustion, mental distance or cynicism about the job, and doing the job less well. It applies only to work. [WHO](https://www.who.int/news/item/28-05-2019-burn-out-an-occupational-phenomenon-international-classification-of-diseases) |
| Stress vs anxiety | Stress is usually a response to an outside cause and eases when it's resolved. Anxiety is a lasting dread that doesn't go away even without a threat, and gets in the way of life. [NIMH](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/so-stressed-out-fact-sheet) |
| Generalised anxiety disorder | Worry about many things that's hard to control, much of the time, for at least six months. [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/generalised-anxiety-disorder/overview/) |
| Depression | See a doctor or nurse if low mood or loss of interest lasts most of the day, every day, for more than 2 weeks. [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/depression-in-adults/symptoms/) |
| When to get help | WHO: when stress symptoms persist, affect daily life at work or school, or cause or worsen anxiety or depression. [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/stress) |

## Calming the body

Checked 2026-11-11.

- **Breathing:** breathe in gently while counting steadily from 1 to 5, then out while counting
  1 to 5. Keep going for at least 5 minutes; it helps most as a daily habit.
  [NHS: breathing exercises for stress](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/breathing-exercises-for-stress/)
- **Movement:** WHO recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults, and
  says activity reduces symptoms of depression and anxiety.
  [WHO: physical activity](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/physical-activity)
- **Sleep:** go to bed and wake at about the same time each day; no caffeine in the afternoon or
  evening; avoid alcohol, smoking, tea and coffee for at least 6 hours before bed; put the phone
  away; no exercise in the 4 hours before bed.
  [NHS Every Mind Matters: sleep](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-health-issues/sleep/)

## Calming the mind

Checked 2026-11-11.

- **Worry time:** set aside 10 or 15 minutes every day or so to write worries down and look for
  solutions. When a worry comes up at other times, tell yourself you'll deal with it then.
  [NHS: tackling your worries](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/tackling-your-worries/)
- **Problem solving:** sort worries into problems you can act on and "what ifs" you can't, tackle
  the solvable ones first, make a plan, then review how it went.
  [NHS: problem solving](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/problem-solving/)
- **WHO's free guide:** [Doing What Matters in Times of Stress](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003927)
  (2020, free, many languages, with audio exercises) teaches five skills: grounding, unhooking
  from difficult thoughts, acting on values, making room for emotions, and being kind.
  [WHO](https://www.who.int/thailand/activities/doing-what-matters-in-times-of-stress)

## Supporting someone

Sources: [NIMH: 5 action steps](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/5-action-steps-to-help-someone-having-thoughts-of-suicide)
and [Samaritans: supporting someone you're worried about](https://www.samaritans.org/how-we-can-help/if-youre-worried-about-someone-else/how-support-someone-youre-worried-about/),
checked 2026-11-11.

- NIMH's five steps: **ask** directly whether they're thinking about suicide; **be there** and
  listen without judging; **help keep them safe**; **help them connect** to a crisis line and
  people they trust; **follow up**.
- NIMH: studies show asking someone if they are suicidal does not increase suicidal thoughts or
  behaviour.
- Samaritans' warning signs: changes in mood, sleep, eating or routine; withdrawing; drinking or
  using drugs more; talking about feeling hopeless, worthless or trapped; taking risks.
