# Managing stress

What stress does to your body and mind, how to tell it from burnout, anxiety and depression,
practical ways to calm down, how to support a friend, and where to get help. Taught by your AI
assistant.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **In crisis, or worried someone is?** Don't use this course; contact a crisis line now.
> South Africa: SADAG Suicide Crisis Helpline **0800 567 567** (free, 24 hours), or **112** from a
> cellphone. UK: Samaritans **116 123**, or **999**. US: call or text **988**, or **911**.
> Other numbers, and the sources they were checked on, are in [facts.md](facts.md).

## This is not therapy or medical advice

This course explains how stress works and teaches general self-help skills from WHO, the NHS and
NIMH. It can't diagnose you or treat you. For your own situation, see a doctor, nurse, clinic or
counsellor.

## Known gaps

- **Help lines for other countries.** Only South Africa, the UK and the US are listed. Elsewhere
  the tutor tells learners to call their local emergency number.
- **Not yet checked on the providers' own sites, so left out:** LifeLine South Africa, Childline
  South Africa, the UK's Shout text line, the US Crisis Text Line, and SAMHSA's helpline number
  (its site refused automated checks; NIMH's page links to it instead).
- **South African emergency numbers** (112, 10111, 10177) were checked on the Western Cape
  Government's site; a national government page would be a better source.
- **Cost of public clinic care in South Africa.** Government pages say clinics offer mental health
  services but don't state the current cost, so the course doesn't claim it is free.
- **Childline UK hours** weren't on the NHS page; the course points to childline.org.uk.
- **Listening tips in lesson 6** beyond NIMH's five steps (open questions, reflecting back) are
  general good practice that a reviewer should confirm against a source.
